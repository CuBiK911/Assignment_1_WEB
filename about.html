The History of Tiramisu

Tiramisu's origins are shrouded in delicious mystery, with both the Veneto and Friuli regions of Italy claiming its invention. Most food historians agree that modern tiramisu was created in the 1960s or 1970s, though some claim earlier versions existed. The dessert gained international fame in the 1980s and has since become a staple of Italian cuisine worldwide. Its name, meaning "pick me up," refers to the energizing combination of sugar and caffeine from the espresso.

Historical Timeline

  1. 1960s: First documented recipes appear in Italian cookbooks from the Veneto region.
  2. 1971: Recipe published in "La Marca del Veneto" magazine.
  3. 1981: Tiramisu gains massive popularity across Italy and Europe.
  4. 1990s: International fame spreads to America and Asia.
  5. 2017: Tiramisu recognized as an official Italian traditional product.

Essential Facts

  • Traditional tiramisu contains raw eggs, though modern versions use pasteurized eggs.
  • The dessert should rest in the refrigerator for at least 6 hours before serving.
  • Authentic recipes never include alcohol, though marsala wine is sometimes added.
  • Tiramisu Day is celebrated on March 21st in Italy.
